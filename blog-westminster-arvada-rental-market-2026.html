Westminster & Arvada rental market 2026: what landlords need to know

We manage rentals in both cities, and in 2026 they're telling two different stories. Here's an approximate, current read on where Westminster and Arvada rents actually stand — and what it means for how you price and manage your property this year.

By Nadir Maaiah·August 11, 2026·6 min read

The short answer

  • Westminster is renting for roughly $2,000 a month on average, with three-bedroom houses often landing $2,700–$3,000-plus.
  • Arvada has softened more — averages sit closer to $1,790–$1,830, down year-over-year by anywhere from a few percent to double digits depending on the source.
  • Westminster is outperforming several nearby suburbs on both values and occupancy, while Arvada's charm and school-district draw are keeping it competitive despite the softer numbers.
  • In both cities, accurate comp-based pricing — not the city line — is what actually determines how fast you rent and what you net this year.

Westminster and Arvada sit next to each other on the map, share a lot of the same renter pool, and get lumped together constantly in "north Denver metro" market coverage. In practice, 2026 has them moving in different directions. Here's an approximate, current-as-of-2026 picture of both markets, grounded in the same data owners are pulling themselves, and what it should mean for how you price and manage your property this year.

Where Westminster rents stand right now

As of early 2026, the average rent across all property types in Westminster runs around $2,000 a month, per Zillow Rental Manager's tracked data. That figure blends everything from smaller condos to larger houses, so it undersells what a well-kept single-family home actually commands — three-bedroom houses typically rent well above the city average, often landing somewhere in the $2,700 to $3,000-plus range depending on size, condition, and how close the property sits to shopping, transit, or the Standley Lake area. Westminster has continued to outperform several nearby suburbs on both property values and occupancy, helped by a central location, decent transit options, and a supply picture that hasn't been flooded with new competing rentals the way parts of Denver proper have.

Where Arvada rents stand right now

Arvada tells a softer story. Depending on which tracker you check, the average rent sits somewhere between $1,790 and $1,830 a month, and — unlike Westminster — that number has actually declined year-over-year. The size of the drop depends heavily on the source: some trackers show a modest 2% pullback, others show double-digit declines closer to 10–13%. Treat the exact percentage skeptically and focus on the direction: Arvada rents have cooled more than Westminster's in the past year. Unit size still matters a great deal — two-bedrooms cluster around $1,600–$1,800, while three-bedroom houses still command a real premium, typically landing near $2,700 or more, not far off Westminster's house pricing.

Why the two cities are diverging

Part of this is the same story playing out across the wider Denver metro: a wave of new apartment construction downtown and along the transit corridors has cooled rents in areas with heavy new supply, while suburbs with little new rental construction have held up better. Westminster's mix — a large share of single-family and townhome rentals with limited new competing supply — has insulated it from a lot of that pressure. We break down the metro-wide version of this dynamic in our Denver rental market 2026 report.

Arvada's softening looks less like a supply glut and more like a market correcting off a couple of very strong pandemic-era years. It's also worth noting that Arvada's average is pulled down by a large share of smaller apartments and condos in the data — the market for a solid three-bedroom house in a good Arvada neighborhood is holding up considerably better than the headline average suggests. Regulatory oversight has tightened across Colorado generally, from fair housing requirements to inspection standards, and that applies evenly to both cities.

What Westminster & Arvada landlords should do in 2026

  • Price to your neighborhood, not the city average. Both averages blend property types that don't compete with each other — a three-bedroom house in Westminster's Legacy Ridge isn't pricing against a one-bedroom apartment near Olde Town Arvada, and shouldn't be priced as if it were.
  • In Arvada especially, don't chase last year's number. With rents having pulled back, pricing to a 2024 or early-2025 comp will sit unrented for weeks while a correctly priced unit down the street fills in days.
  • Focus on retention. Turnover costs are the same whether the market is rising or softening — a fair renewal to a good tenant beats a vacancy and a re-lease almost every time. We cover the real math in what tenant turnover actually costs.
  • Keep the home genuinely move-in ready. With more renter choice in a cooler market, condition and presentation are doing more of the work to justify your asking rent.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average rent in Westminster, CO in 2026?

Roughly $2,000 a month across all property types, per Zillow Rental Manager data from early 2026. Three-bedroom houses typically rent well above that, often $2,700–$3,000-plus.

What is the average rent in Arvada, CO in 2026?

Roughly $1,790 to $1,830 a month depending on the source, and rents have declined year-over-year — estimates of the drop range from about 2% to over 10%. Three-bedroom houses still command a premium, typically around $2,700 or more.

Is Westminster or Arvada a better rental market for landlords right now?

Both are workable. Westminster's rents have held up better and the city continues to outperform several nearby suburbs on values and occupancy. Arvada has softened more on paper, but its walkability and school-district reputation keep demand steady for well-priced properties. Accurate, comp-based pricing matters more than the city line in either case.
