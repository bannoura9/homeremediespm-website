How to winterize a Denver-metro rental before the first hard freeze

The houses that flood are usually vacant, or they still have a hose on the bib. Heat stays on, sprinklers get blown out, and a no-heat call does not wait on a contractor's calendar.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·November 21, 2026·8 min read

The short answer

  • Leave the heat on, including in a vacant unit. Turning the system off to save a utility bill is how supply lines freeze.
  • Blow out sprinklers before the first hard freeze, disconnect hoses, and drain hose bibs. A hose left attached bursts the pipe inside the wall.
  • On a deep cold night, open the cabinet doors on exterior-wall sinks and let a vulnerable faucet drip. That is field practice, not a statute.
  • If the heat or the water fails while someone is living there, you are on the habitability clock.

Front Range rentals do not get a mild winter. A week of chinook warmth followed by a night in the single digits is a normal January from Westminster and Arvada through Thornton, Northglenn, Broomfield, and north Denver. The houses that flood are usually the ones that were "empty for just a bit" or still had a garden hose threaded on the bib. This is the owner's list for that season. It does not invent loss statistics. The annual version of the same work is our rental maintenance calendar.

Heat stays on, occupied or not

If a tenant is in the house, heat is not optional. Colorado's warranty of habitability treats working heat as part of a fit place to live, and a no-heat call in winter is the fact pattern our habitability guide is written around. The response window for a condition that is immediately dangerous is measured in hours. "The HVAC company is booked until Thursday" does not pause it. Service the furnace in the fall, change the filter, and ask the tenant in December whether the house is actually warm. A thermostat reading in the hallway does not tell you the bedrooms are warm.

A vacant unit needs the heat left on too. Pipes in a crawl space, a garage, an exterior wall, or an unheated utility room freeze while the rest of the house still feels fine. Do not turn the system off between tenants. Do not set it and leave town without confirming the system is actually running. If the thermostat is in a sunny interior hall, the sensor can be satisfied while the north-wall bathroom is not. Know where the main water shutoff is, and make sure whoever is watching the house knows too.

There is no single statewide thermostat number in the materials this guide relies on, so this page will not invent one. Westminster's rental code requires heating as part of the minimum standards for a rental and does not, in the version reviewed, print a degree setpoint. Follow the equipment maker's cold-weather guidance, keep the house warm enough that pipes in the coldest room stay above freezing, and document that the system was on. If you want a number for a specific house, ask your HVAC contractor, not a blog.

Water that should not be under pressure

Disconnect every hose and drain the exterior bibs before the first hard freeze. A hose left on the faucet holds water in the line inside the wall. That is the pipe that splits, often where you cannot see it until the thaw. Insulated bib covers help. They do not fix a hose that is still attached.

Blow out the sprinkler system, including the backflow preventer, before that same freeze. "When it gets cold" is late. Our maintenance calendar treats this as the highest-priority fall item for a reason: an undrained system cracks parts you then dig up in March. If you are not equipped to blow out a system, hire an irrigation company. Do the same for a swamp cooler: drain it, disconnect and drain the supply line, and cover the unit. A live line to an uncovered cooler is a burst waiting for a cold snap.

During a deep cold snap, open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room air reaches the traps and supply lines. For a faucet on a pipe that has frozen before, a slow drip is a common plumber's practice so water keeps moving. It is not a city ordinance, and it is not a reason to leave every tap in the house running for a week. Use it on the vulnerable fixtures, tell the tenant why, and watch the water bill. If a pipe has already frozen, do not pour boiling water on it or fire up a torch. Shut the water off if you can do that safely and call a plumber. Thawing a split line from the wrong side is how a freeze becomes a flood.

The walk, the roof edge, and whose job the snow is

Ice on the walk to the front door is both a habitability and safety problem and a premises-liability problem. Clear the path your tenant, their guests, and the mail carrier actually use. Many Denver-metro cities require adjacent sidewalks to be cleared, and the deadline is not the same in every city. Check this year's page for the city where the house sits rather than borrowing a rule from the city next door. If the lease says the tenant shovels, the lease does not repeal a municipal sidewalk rule that names the owner, and it does not shovel the walk while the tenant is out of town. Decide in the fall who is on call, and write it down.

After a heavy snow followed by sun, look at the roof edge. Icicles and an ice dam are a warning that melt water is backing up under the shingles. That water shows up on a ceiling, not in the gutter. Our maintenance calendar covers the gutter cleaning that prevents a lot of this. Do it after leaf drop, not during the storm.

When the heat or the water actually fails

Treat a tenant report of no heat, no water, or a leak from a burst line as a habitability notice. Acknowledge it in writing, and start the repair. The obligations and the timelines are in the habitability guide. A space heater is a bridge, not a cure, and a combustion heater a tenant brings in from the garage is its own carbon-monoxide risk. If you supply a temporary heater, use a listed electric unit, keep it on a hard surface, and do not treat it as the permanent heat.

If the house is vacant and a pipe lets go, the loss is yours: water, drywall, and the weeks the house cannot be shown. This page will not put a dollar figure on that. The comparison that matters is qualitative and obvious. The utility bill you avoided by shutting the heat off is smaller than opening a wall in February. Preventive steps are listed month by month in the maintenance calendar, including the fall furnace service that keeps you off the January emergency list.

A short fall checklist

  • Furnace serviced, filter changed, thermostat confirmed to be running the system.
  • Hoses off, bibs drained, covers on.
  • Sprinklers and backflow blown out. Swamp cooler drained, line disconnected, unit covered.
  • Gutters clear after the leaves.
  • Shutoff located. Tenant or local contact has the number and knows to report no-heat the day it happens.
  • Snow plan written down, including who clears the walk if the tenant is away.
  • Vacant units left with the heat on. Nobody "winterizes" an occupied rental by turning the furnace off.

If the house is in Westminster, the city's rental code already expects heating as a minimum standard, and a complaint can bring an inspector whether or not you are on the systematic schedule. That program is in our Westminster rental license guide. Keeping the house warm is cheaper than explaining a frozen pipe to either the tenant or the city.

Frequently asked questions

Should I leave the heat on in a vacant Colorado rental?

Yes. Pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and exterior walls freeze in vacant houses when the system is off. Confirm the heat is actually running before you leave.

What temperature does Colorado law require?

This guide did not find a single statewide setpoint to quote, and it will not invent one. Habitability requires working heat. Westminster's rental code requires heating without stating a degree number in the version reviewed. Ask your HVAC contractor what this house needs to keep the coldest pipes above freezing.

When do I blow out sprinklers?

Before the first hard freeze, not after the first cold week. Disconnect hoses and drain hose bibs in the same window. The maintenance calendar covers the rest of the fall list.

What if the tenant says there is no heat?

Treat it as a habitability notice. Respond in writing and start the repair. The timelines are in the habitability guide. A booked contractor is not a defense.
