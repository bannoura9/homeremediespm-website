Golden, Lakewood & Wheat Ridge rental market 2026: what landlords need to know

These three west-metro cities sit right next to each other along the foothills, but 2026 has them at three genuinely different price points. Here's a current, approximate read on where each market actually stands, and what it means for pricing your rental this year.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·September 10, 2026·7 min read

The short answer

  • Golden is the priciest of the three — roughly $2,000-$2,190 a month blended, still down about 6% year-over-year, though the decline has slowed sharply compared to 2025.
  • Lakewood sits in the middle — roughly $1,700-$1,790, down a modest 3-4% year-over-year, a gradual softening rather than a sharp correction.
  • Wheat Ridge is the most affordable and the most stable — roughly $1,670-$1,780, essentially flat year-over-year.
  • All three are real, active rental markets — three major regional property managers maintain dedicated pages for each city, and one of the biggest (Keyrenter Denver) is headquartered in Wheat Ridge itself.

Golden, Lakewood, and Wheat Ridge sit along the same stretch of the west metro, sharing the foothills backdrop and a lot of the same renter pool — but 2026 has sorted them into three distinct price tiers. Here's an approximate, current-as-of-2026 picture of all three, and what it should mean for how you price and manage a rental in any of them.

Where Golden rents stand right now

Golden is the most expensive of the three by a clear margin. Blended averages run $2,000 to $2,190 a month, with the citywide median closer to $1,700 — the gap between those numbers reflects Golden's mix of larger, newer units alongside older, smaller ones. Year-over-year, Golden is still down roughly 6%, continuing a correction from a stronger 2025. But the pace has slowed considerably: rents are up about 1.5% so far this year, versus a 6.1% climb over the same stretch last year. One-bedrooms are down about 5.5% and two-bedrooms about 3.6% year-over-year. Read that as a market finding its floor, not one still falling fast.

Where Lakewood rents stand right now

Lakewood sits squarely in the middle of the three. Average rent runs around $1,700 to $1,790, with the median nearer $1,618. Year-over-year, Lakewood is down a more modest 3-4% — a gradual softening rather than the sharper pullback Golden saw. By unit size, studios run near $1,210, one-bedrooms around $1,601, two-bedrooms around $1,939, and three-bedrooms around $2,403. Lakewood runs about 13% below the national average rent overall.

Where Wheat Ridge rents stand right now

Wheat Ridge is both the most affordable of the three and the most stable. Averages run $1,670 to $1,780, with the median closer to $1,499. Unlike Golden and Lakewood, Wheat Ridge is essentially flat year-over-year — some trackers show it up slightly (around 0.2%), others show no change at all. One-bedrooms average near $1,388 and two-bedrooms near $1,555, with three- and four-bedroom units climbing more steeply from there. Wheat Ridge also runs about 13% below the national average.

Why these three markets are moving differently

Golden's higher price point and sharper correction both trace back to the same thing: a housing stock skewed toward newer, larger units that saw steeper run-ups in 2024-2025 and now have further to fall as that demand normalizes. Lakewood's larger, more varied inventory is cooling more gradually because it was never as overheated to begin with. Wheat Ridge's flat trend reflects a market that's already priced close to its natural level — smaller, older housing stock with steady, unspectacular demand.

All three are genuinely active rental markets, not afterthoughts. Regional property managers — the same firms operating throughout the north and west metro — maintain dedicated pages for all three cities, and Keyrenter Denver's own office sits in Wheat Ridge. That's a much better signal of real demand than any single rent figure.

What Golden, Lakewood & Wheat Ridge landlords should do in 2026

  • In Golden, don't panic-price off last year's decline. The correction has slowed noticeably — pull current comps before assuming the market is still falling as fast as it was.
  • In Lakewood, match your comp to your unit size closely. The spread between a studio and a three-bedroom here is wide enough that a blended average will mislead you either direction.
  • In Wheat Ridge, stability is your friend. Older comps stay useful longer here than in Golden or Lakewood — but that also means underpricing out of habit costs you more over time.
  • Across all three, focus on retention. Turnover costs the same regardless of which way the market is trending — a fair renewal to a good tenant beats a vacancy and re-lease almost every time. We cover the math in what tenant turnover actually costs.
