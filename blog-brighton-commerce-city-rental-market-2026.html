Brighton & Commerce City rental market 2026: what landlords need to know

Both sit northeast of Denver, both get lumped together by owners scouting where to buy next — but the numbers tell different stories. Here's a current, approximate read on where Brighton and Commerce City rents actually stand, and what it means for how you price and manage your property this year.

By Nadir Maaiah·August 24, 2026·6 min read

The short answer

  • Brighton is renting for roughly $1,750–$2,000 a month blended across property types, essentially flat to down slightly year-over-year — a steady, predictable market.
  • Commerce City has a much wider spread — roughly $1,260 for a one-bedroom up to $1,850 for a two-bedroom, with blended city-wide figures ranging even higher depending on how much newer housing stock a tracker includes.
  • The gap in Commerce City comes down to housing mix: older, more affordable stock sits alongside newer, pricier development, and the two pull the average in different directions.
  • National and regional property managers already market actively in both cities — real evidence of live rental demand, which makes accurate, unit-specific pricing worth getting right rather than leaning on a citywide number.

Brighton and Commerce City both sit northeast of Denver and get compared constantly by owners weighing where to buy next — reasonable, since they're neighbors with overlapping renter pools. In practice, 2026 has them behaving differently. Brighton is the more settled of the two; Commerce City's numbers move around a lot more depending on which slice of its housing stock you're looking at. Here's an approximate, current-as-of-2026 picture of both markets, and what it should mean for how you price and manage your property this year.

Where Brighton rents stand right now

As of 2026, the average rent across all property types in Brighton runs somewhere around $1,750 to $2,000 a month, per Rent.com and Rent Cafe tracked data. Year-over-year, the market is essentially flat to down slightly — some trackers show around a 2% pullback, which is mild compared to some of its north-metro neighbors. One-bedroom units average close to $1,765, and unlike some nearby markets, Brighton hasn't seen the same wide month-to-month swings by unit size. That relative steadiness makes it an easier market to price against — comps from a few months ago are still mostly reliable today.

Where Commerce City rents stand right now

Commerce City is a different story, and it's worth understanding why before you trust any single number. Broken out by unit size, one-bedrooms average around $1,260 a month and two-bedrooms around $1,850, per Zumper. But blended, citywide "average rent" figures range much more widely across trackers — from roughly $1,820 on the low end to $2,800 on the high end, depending heavily on how much newer, larger single-family and townhome inventory a given source folds into its number. Treat any single blended average with real skepticism here. Year-over-year direction is mixed too, from modestly up by some measures to modestly down by others. The practical takeaway: Commerce City's rental stock is genuinely bifurcated — older, more affordable housing on one side, newer and pricier development on the other — and a citywide average blurs the two into a number that doesn't describe any specific property well.

Why the two cities are behaving differently

Brighton's housing stock skews more consistent in age and size, which keeps its rent distribution tighter and its year-over-year moves smaller. It's also benefited from steady demand as renters priced out of closer-in metro submarkets look further northeast. We cover the wider version of that dynamic in our Denver rental market 2026 report.

Commerce City's wider spread traces back to how much new construction has landed there in recent years, layered on top of an older, more affordable existing housing base. That's exactly the kind of market where two "average rent" figures from two different sources can both be technically correct and still be useless for pricing a specific unit. It's also a market real competitors are actively targeting — regional and national property managers maintain dedicated Commerce City and Brighton pages, which is a reasonable signal that rental demand in both cities is real and ongoing, not a fluke of the data.

What Brighton & Commerce City landlords should do in 2026

  • In Commerce City, price off your specific unit type and neighborhood, not a citywide number. The gap between a one-bedroom and a newer two-bedroom in this market is too wide for a blended average to be useful.
  • In Brighton, trust recent comps more than usual. The market's relative stability means older data stays useful longer here than in more volatile submarkets.
  • Don't assume "average rent" means the same thing across sources. Before setting a price off any single report, check whether it's blending house and apartment stock, and across which bedroom counts.
  • Focus on retention. Turnover costs are the same regardless of which direction the market is moving — a fair renewal to a good tenant beats a vacancy and a re-lease almost every time. We cover the real math in what tenant turnover actually costs.
