Short-term rental rules in Westminster, Arvada, and Denver

Three north-metro cities, three different permissions. Westminster caps you at one premise, Arvada rewrote its ordinance in 2026, and Denver requires the host to live there.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·November 21, 2026·8 min read

The short answer

  • Westminster: under 30 days, a city license, one licensed premise per applicant, no initial fee, a two-year term, and a $200 renewal plus processing. HOA compliance is on you. Sales tax and accommodations tax are separate.
  • Arvada: Ordinance 4917, effective May 1, 2026, requires a permit and a business license. Stays of 30 or more consecutive days are outside that section. A natural person may hold up to three permits, including a non-primary residence.
  • Denver: a license, the host's primary residence, the license number on every ad, plus tax, zoning, and insurance rules. Hosts who do not follow them can be fined or lose the license.
  • Boulder is a different code. These three cities change their pages. Confirm on the city site before you list.

Booking a weekend stay? This page is for owners. The license has to exist before the ad does. If the stay you actually want is a month or more, read the mid-term furnished rental guide alongside this one. Boulder owners should use the Boulder short-term rental guide instead of assuming these rules travel.

Short-term rental rules in Westminster, Arvada, and Denver are municipal. A state tax account, an HOA "ok," or a neighboring city's permit does not substitute. The pages below were read for this guide. Cities edit them. If this article and the live page disagree, the live page wins, and you should re-read it before you take a booking. Nothing here is legal advice.

Westminster: one license, under 30 days

The City of Westminster's short-term rental page says a short-term rental is a residential dwelling, or part of one, used for less than 30 days. Hotels, motels, and bed and breakfasts are excluded. The examples include a single-family home, duplex, townhome, or condo. Anyone offering that lodging has to obtain and maintain a city license first.

No applicant may operate more than one licensed premise in the city at a time. HOA rules are the owner's problem. The page says to confirm that short-term rentals are allowed in the neighborhood. City approval is not HOA approval. Our HOA guide covers how to read the covenants.

Initial applications, the city says, do not require a registration fee and expire after two years. Renewal is $200 plus a processing fee. The application asks for a narrative or visual of which parts of the dwelling will be used, a copy of the recorded deed of trust, and a signed life-safety affidavit covering operational smoke detectors, carbon monoxide detectors, fire extinguishers, and other equipment the building, fire, or rental code requires. "Recorded deed of trust" is the phrase on the page. Owners often have a deed, not a deed of trust, in the file. Ask the city which document the portal will accept before you assume a warranty deed is enough.

The same page requires a business and sales tax license and accommodations tax, collected through the finance department. Tax questions go to salestax@westminsterco.gov or 303-658-2065. The page also lists business-license contacts at licensing@westminsterco.gov, and it prints more than one phone number for that desk, including 303-658-2400 and 303-658-2008. Use the number on the live page the day you call. Short-term questions go to str@westminsterco.gov or 303-658-2342. The 24/7 complaint line published there is 303-867-7363.

A stay that is not under 30 days is outside this license and may still need Westminster rental registration or a rental license. That split is in our Westminster rental license guide.

Arvada: permit, business license, and a 2026 ordinance

Ordinance 4917, Council Bill 26-001, was adopted February 17, 2026, and the ordinance says it is effective May 1, 2026. It replaced Arvada's older short-term section. If you are operating from a summary written before that date, retire it.

The ordinance defines a short-term rental as a furnished dwelling, or part of one, rented for less than 30 consecutive calendar days, counting portions of days as full calendar days. It says nothing in the section precludes renting a dwelling for 30 or more consecutive days. The program rules describe the short-term period as 2 to 29 consecutive days, and the ordinance makes a rental of fewer than two consecutive days unlawful. "31 nights" is not the phrase either document uses. Because partial days count, confirm a checkout morning with the city before you treat a stay as exempt.

Each property offered as a short-term rental needs an STR permit, and the permittee needs a city business license while the permit is in effect. A permit may be for a primary residence or a non-primary residence. That is a real difference from Denver. The permittee is generally one natural person, 18 or older, with an ownership interest of at least 20 percent, or the named beneficiary of a trust that holds the property. An entity that already held a permit on May 1, 2026, may keep it for that term. At renewal the entity has to name a natural person. A lapsed, revoked, or transferred permit has to be reissued to a natural person. No person may hold more than three STR permits. Only one permit may be issued per lot.

The permit number has to be on every ad. The permit lasts one year, is not transferable, and ends if ownership falls below 20 percent or the property is sold. Lodging and sales taxes have to be paid. The rules require proof of insurance, and the director may set minimums with the city's risk staff. Deed-restricted affordable housing may not be a short-term rental. Occupancy is capped at two per bedroom plus two, or three in a studio, and that maximum goes in the ad. No STR may be occupied more than 240 days in any 365-day period. Parking, snow and trash, an emergency contact, and limits on indoor cameras are in the ordinance and the rules. Fees are whatever the fee schedule says when you apply. This is a map, not a substitute for those pages.

Denver: primary residence, license on the ad

Denver's laws and rules page states the features in plain language. Every short-term rental must have a license. The rental must be the host's primary residence. Every advertisement must display the business license number. Hosts must follow the rules on safety, taxes, zoning, and insurance. Hosts who do not follow the rules could be fined or could lose the license.

The city's short-term rental overview adds the day count, and the wording is not perfectly uniform. It says a short-term rental is a dwelling available for one to 29 days, that fewer than 29 nights at one time requires the short-term license, and that renting only for 30-plus days requires a residential rental license instead. Primary residence, on that page, is the place where the person's habitation is fixed for the license term and the person's usual place of return. A person can have only one. The 2020 ordinance text Denver posts defines a short-term rental as lodging for less than thirty consecutive days. The finance department's short-term rental tax guide, revised April 2026, uses "less than 30 consecutive days" as well. If your booking is near that line, ask licensing which product you are in. Do not average the phrases.

You do not get a Denver short-term license for an investment condo you do not live in. A non-owner host needs the owner's permission, and the home still has to be that host's primary residence. Zoning, insurance, and tax are separate duties on the laws page, and the ordinance, rules, tax, and zoning PDFs linked there control the details this summary skips.

What is the same in all three, and what is not

All three require permission before the ad, collect lodging taxes a normal lease does not, and leave HOA bans in place. None of them is Boulder. Westminster allows one licensed premise and charges nothing up front. Arvada allows a non-primary home and up to three permits, with a 240-day cap and a natural-person rule. Denver requires the host to live there. A portfolio that is lawful in Arvada can be unlawful in Denver on the same facts.

Arvada's ordinance already changed in 2026, so re-check the city page in the season you list. Help running a lawful short-term program starts at STR management. If the stay is a month or longer, use the mid-term guide, and request a rental analysis before you furnish around an unconfirmed license.

Frequently asked questions

How many short-term rentals can I license in Westminster?

The city's page says no applicant may operate more than one licensed premise as a short-term rental in Westminster at a time.

What does a Westminster short-term license cost?

The city says the initial application has no registration fee and expires after two years. Renewal is $200 plus a processing fee. Sales tax licensing and accommodations tax are separate.

Can I short-term rent an Arvada house I do not live in?

Ordinance 4917 says a permit may be for a primary or a non-primary residence. Other limits still apply, including the natural-person rule, a cap of three permits, one permit per lot, and the 240-day occupancy cap.

Can I short-term rent a Denver investment property?

Denver's laws page says any short-term rental must be the host's primary residence. A host who is not the owner needs the owner's permission, and the home still has to be that host's primary residence.

Do these rules apply in Boulder?

No. Boulder writes its own ordinance. Use the Boulder short-term rental guide and the City of Boulder's current page.
