Broomfield & Federal Heights rental market 2026: what landlords need to know

We manage rentals in both cities, and in 2026 they're telling two different stories. Here's a current, approximate read on where Broomfield and Federal Heights rents actually stand — and what it means for how you price and manage your property this year.

By Nadir Maaiah·August 17, 2026·6 min read

The short answer

  • Broomfield is renting for roughly $1,950–$2,000 a month blended across property types, down modestly year-over-year (around 3%) — one of the more stable markets in the north metro.
  • Federal Heights has softened more — averages sit closer to $1,400–$1,700, with some trackers showing year-over-year declines near 10%.
  • Broomfield's stability is helped by its position between two job centers — Denver and Boulder — while Federal Heights remains the most affordable option in the immediate area.
  • In both cities, accurate comp-based pricing — not the city line — is what actually determines how fast you rent and what you net this year.

Broomfield and Federal Heights sit on opposite sides of Westminster, share a lot of the same renter pool as the rest of the north Denver metro, and get compared constantly by owners weighing where to buy next. In practice, 2026 has them sitting at different price points and moving at different speeds. Here's an approximate, current-as-of-2026 picture of both markets, and what it should mean for how you price and manage your property this year.

Where Broomfield rents stand right now

As of 2026, the average rent across all property types in Broomfield runs somewhere around $1,950 to $2,000 a month, per Zillow Rental Manager and Rent Cafe tracked data. That's held up better than several neighboring suburbs — the median has only pulled back around 3% year-over-year, and about half of all rentals citywide fall in the $1,500–$2,000 range. Unit size still drives most of the spread — studios average around $1,757 and one-bedrooms around $1,807, while two-bedroom units typically land closer to $2,300 or more. Broomfield's stability owes a lot to its geography: it sits between the Denver and Boulder job markets, giving it a renter pool that isn't solely dependent on either city's employment swings.

Where Federal Heights rents stand right now

Federal Heights tells a softer story. Depending on which tracker and month you check, the average rent lands somewhere between $1,400 and $1,700 a month, and — unlike Broomfield — that number has pulled back more sharply. Some trackers show year-over-year declines approaching 10%. Treat the exact percentage skeptically and focus on the direction: Federal Heights has cooled more than its neighbors this year. It's also the most consistently affordable market in the immediate area — about 72% of rentals citywide are priced between $1,001 and $1,500, with one-bedrooms averaging around $1,270 and two-bedrooms around $1,522.

Why the two cities are diverging

Broomfield's position — walkable to two major employment corridors, with a housing mix that includes a healthy share of newer construction — has kept demand steadier than in markets more exposed to a single job center. We break down the metro-wide version of this dynamic in our Denver rental market 2026 report.

Federal Heights' softening looks less like a supply glut and more like renters shopping around in a market where they have more options than they did a couple of years ago. It's a smaller, older housing stock overall, which is part of why it remains the value option in the cluster — a well-kept property here can still stand out. Regulatory oversight has tightened across Colorado generally, from fair housing requirements to inspection standards, and that applies evenly across both cities.

What Broomfield & Federal Heights landlords should do in 2026

  • Price to your neighborhood and unit size, not the city average. A two-bedroom in a newer Broomfield community isn't pricing against a one-bedroom in an older Federal Heights building, and the blended averages hide that gap.
  • In Federal Heights especially, lean into value positioning. With rents having pulled back more here, a well-presented unit priced to current comps will lease faster than one priced off last year's numbers.
  • In Broomfield, don't underprice out of habit. The market has held up better than most of the metro — owners who haven't re-run comps recently may be leaving rent on the table.
  • Focus on retention. Turnover costs are the same whether the market is rising or softening — a fair renewal to a good tenant beats a vacancy and a re-lease almost every time. We cover the real math in what tenant turnover actually costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average rent in Broomfield, CO in 2026?

Roughly $1,950 to $2,000 a month across all property types, down modestly year-over-year (around 3%). Two-bedroom units typically run closer to $2,300.

What is the average rent in Federal Heights, CO in 2026?
