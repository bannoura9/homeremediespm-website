Thornton & Northglenn rental market 2026: what landlords need to know

We manage rentals in both cities, and in 2026 they're telling two different stories. Here's a current, approximate read on where Thornton and Northglenn rents actually stand — and what it means for how you price and manage your property this year.

By Nadir Maaiah·August 12, 2026·6 min read

The short answer

  • Thornton is renting for roughly $2,450–$2,500 a month blended across property types, and that number has kept climbing — some trackers show year-over-year increases around 11%.
  • Northglenn has softened — averages sit closer to $1,540–$1,800 depending on the month and source, down roughly 5% year-over-year on some trackers.
  • Thornton's growth is being driven by continued population growth and a rental mix weighted toward single-family homes, while Northglenn's smaller, older housing stock is more exposed to renter price-shopping in a cooler stretch.
  • In both cities, accurate comp-based pricing — not the city line — is what actually determines how fast you rent and what you net this year.

Thornton and Northglenn sit directly north of our Westminster home base, share a border, and get treated as one interchangeable submarket in a lot of casual market talk. In practice, 2026 has them moving in different directions. Here's an approximate, current-as-of-2026 picture of both markets, and what it should mean for how you price and manage your property this year.

Where Thornton rents stand right now

As of 2026, the average rent across all property types in Thornton runs somewhere around $2,450 to $2,500 a month, per Zillow Rental Manager's tracked data — roughly 24% above the national average. That blended figure includes everything from smaller apartments to full single-family houses, and the spread between them is wide: standalone apartments alone typically run $1,450 to $1,900 depending on unit size, while houses pull the city-wide average up considerably higher. Unlike a lot of the metro, Thornton's rents have kept climbing rather than cooling — some trackers put the year-over-year increase around 11%, driven by the city's continued population growth and a housing mix that leans heavily toward single-family and townhome rentals rather than large new apartment complexes.

Where Northglenn rents stand right now

Northglenn tells a softer story. Depending on which tracker and month you check, the average rent lands somewhere between $1,540 and $1,800 a month, and several sources show that number having declined year-over-year — one tracker puts the drop near 5%. Northglenn's housing stock skews older and smaller than Thornton's, with a larger share of the market made up of condos and smaller apartments, which is part of why the blended average sits meaningfully lower. Unit size still matters a great deal here too — two-bedrooms cluster in the $1,700s, while three-bedroom units still command a real premium, often landing north of $2,700.

Why the two cities are diverging

Part of the split is simple supply and demand. Thornton has continued adding population and rooftops at a pace that's kept demand for its largely single-family rental stock ahead of supply, insulating it from the softening that's shown up in parts of the metro with heavier new apartment construction. We break down the metro-wide version of that dynamic in our Denver rental market 2026 report.

Northglenn's softening looks less like a supply glut and more like an older, smaller-unit market correcting as renters have more options to shop around. It's also worth noting that Northglenn's average is pulled down by a larger share of condos and small apartments in the data — a well-kept three- or four-bedroom house in a good Northglenn neighborhood is holding up considerably better than the headline average suggests. Neighboring Federal Heights sits in a similar spot, with rents generally in the $1,400–$1,700 range and the same dynamic at play. Regulatory oversight has tightened across Colorado generally, from fair housing requirements to inspection standards, and that applies evenly across all three cities.

What Thornton & Northglenn landlords should do in 2026

  • Price to your neighborhood and property type, not the city average. A three-bedroom house in Thornton isn't pricing against a one-bedroom apartment, and the blended city averages hide that gap in both directions.
  • In Northglenn especially, don't chase last year's number. With rents having pulled back, pricing to an early-2025 comp will sit unrented for weeks while a correctly priced unit down the street fills in days.
  • In Thornton, don't underprice out of habit either. Owners who haven't re-run comps in a year or two are often leaving real rent on the table given how much the market has moved.
  • Focus on retention. Turnover costs are the same whether the market is rising or softening — a fair renewal to a good tenant beats a vacancy and a re-lease almost every time. We cover the real math in what tenant turnover actually costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average rent in Thornton, CO in 2026?

Roughly $2,450 to $2,500 a month across all property types, per Zillow Rental Manager data, and that number has been climbing — some trackers show year-over-year increases around 11%. Apartments alone run lower, often $1,450–$1,900.

What is the average rent in Northglenn, CO in 2026?

Roughly $1,540 to $1,800 a month depending on the source and month, and rents have softened — down close to 5% year-over-year on some trackers. Three-bedroom units still command a premium, often above $2,700.

Is Thornton or Northglenn a better rental market for landlords right now?

Both work, but they're moving in opposite directions. Thornton has kept climbing on continued population growth and a single-family-heavy rental mix. Northglenn has cooled somewhat, meaning correctly priced units there lease faster than owners expect if they're pricing off last year's numbers. Comp-based pricing matters more than the city line in either case.
