Licensing

Westminster's rental license and registration, without flattening the city's wording

Westminster sorts a license for four or more units from a registration for a house, a duplex, or up to three condos, and then says every rental must do both. Here is how to read that.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·November 21, 2026·8 min read

The short answer

  • Westminster's inspections page sorts licenses (four or more units, or four or more under one owner in a townhome or condo community) from registrations (a house, a duplex, or up to three townhomes or condos under one owner).
  • The same page also says all residential rentals must be licensed, and all must be registered. Those sentences are broader than the split. The live page wins.
  • The license fee on that page is $50 per rental property. Inspection is $40 per unit, or $20 if the unit is income-restricted. Registration is described as one time, with no fee.
  • The city's application forms require a local agent within 50 miles if the owner does not live or keep a permanent business within that distance.

Renting in Westminster? This is the owner's checklist. Tenants with a housing complaint should use the city's rental housing contact below, not this firm. If you want the home managed after it is legal to lease, start with a free rental analysis or the Westminster property management page.

Westminster is particular about the difference between a rental license and a rental registration, and then its own page states the rule more broadly than that difference. This guide quotes both and does not flatten them. It is not legal advice. The pages cited were read for this article. If they have changed, the live page controls. A wider look at other cities is in our rental license by city guide.

Two tracks, and two sentences that are wider

The city's rental property inspections page says residential rental properties with four or more rental units, or four or more units under single ownership in a townhome or condominium community, are required to be licensed. In the next sentence it says: "All residential rental properties in the city must be licensed."

The registration section says individual townhomes and condominiums, up to three units under a single ownership in any one community, plus duplexes and single-family units, are required to be registered. In the next sentence it says: "All residential rental properties in the city must be registered."

Read those pairs the way they are written. The page gives a specific license trigger and a specific registration category, and it also states a universal license sentence and a universal registration sentence. This guide will not tell you the second sentence is "just boilerplate." Email rentalhousing@westminsterco.gov or call 303-706-3026 and ask which track your address is on. Do that before you advertise. The Residential Rental Code PDF, version dated January 22, 2026, uses the code's own terms: section 8-2-3 requires a rental property license under Title V, Chapter 12, before a rental property is leased, and section 8-2-4 requires a rental dwelling registration under Title VIII, Chapter 1, before a rental dwelling is leased. The PDF does not, in those sections, restate the four-unit test. The inspections page is where the city explains which buildings it puts in each bucket.

Fees, and what "no inspection" does not mean

On the inspections page, the license fee is $50 per rental property. License renewals run with the systematic inspection schedule. The inspection fee is $40 per unit, and $20 per unit if the unit is income-restricted, due before the license is issued.

Registration, on that page, is necessary only once for each rental dwelling unit. The page says there is no registration fee and that no inspections will be required for rental dwelling units. The same page also says single-family homes, duplexes, and up to three townhomes or condos under one owner "are inspected on a complaint-only basis, or can be inspected on a proactive basis." Hold both sentences. "No inspections will be required" in the registration blurb is not a promise that the city will never come. The code matches the cautious reading: section 8-2-5 says rental dwellings are inspected on a complaint basis or as conditions warrant, with no regular inspection schedule.

The inspections page says systematic inspection applies when the property is residential, six years old or older, and either has four or more units or is four or more dwellings under one owner in a townhome or condo community. The January 22, 2026 code sets the calendar for rental properties: under six years, not scheduled; six to twenty years, every four years; older than twenty, every two years. The city can inspect sooner when a property is not kept in decent, safe, and sanitary condition, and it can stretch the gap by no more than two years and never past six years between inspections. Complaints are separate. The code also lists a $50 reinspection fee per unit and common area after a failed first reinspection, and a $50 no-show fee. Council can change both.

A local agent if you are more than 50 miles away

The 50-mile rule is on the city's forms, which are the documents owners actually sign. The rental property license application and the rental dwelling registration application, both city PDFs, say Westminster requires every owner to assign a local agent to receive service of process. An owner who lives in Westminster, or who lives or keeps a permanent business within fifty miles of the rental, may name themselves. An owner who does not must designate a local agent who resides or operates a permanent business inside that fifty-mile radius. The forms say a local agent may include a family member, a friend, or a tenant, and they require proof of authority when a manager or agent signs. The license form cites Westminster Municipal Code section 5-12-5 for the owner's attestations. The residential rental code PDF reviewed for this guide does not itself contain the fifty-mile sentence. If the form and a future code amendment ever differ, use the document the city is enforcing that week and ask rental housing which one controls.

An out-of-state heir cannot leave a Westminster rental with no one local to accept notice. Our out-of-state landlord guide covers the operating side. Naming an agent on the form is not the same as hiring a manager, and hiring a manager does not file the form.

What the code is actually inspecting for

The inspections page says the rental property maintenance code sets minimums for basic equipment, light, ventilation and heating, fire safety, space, and sanitary maintenance. The code's purpose statement matches that list. Before a tenant moves in, confirm smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, heat, hot water, and a weathertight shell. Those items overlap Colorado's warranty of habitability, covered in our habitability guide. A city inspection and a habitability notice are different clocks. Passing one does not answer the other.

The code makes the owner or agent responsible for telling tenants about a scheduled inspection and for getting permission to enter. Without that permission the city does not just walk in. The code describes a court-order process. Put the inspection date on your calendar and on the tenant's. A missed appointment is the no-show fee above, and it does not make the inspection go away.

Short-term rentals, under 30 days, are a separate Westminster license on a separate page. Do not file the long-term registration and assume it covers Airbnb-style stays. That license is covered in our short-term rules guide.

Who to contact

Rental housing: rentalhousing@westminsterco.gov, 303-706-3026, 4800 W. 92nd Avenue, Westminster, CO 80031. The license and registration PDFs linked from the inspections page are the applications. Forms in circulation when this guide was written include the license application and the registration application posted in the city's document center. Use the copy the inspections page links today. City hall's general line, printed on the same page, is 303-658-2400.

File first, then market. An ad for a house that is not registered, or a fourplex that is not licensed, is the expensive order of operations. If you want the ongoing work handled after the city paperwork is in, the Westminster management page is the local starting point, and the pricing page is where fees are published. This article does not restate them.

Frequently asked questions

Does a Westminster single-family rental need a license or a registration?

The inspections page puts single-family homes, duplexes, and up to three townhomes or condos under one owner in the registration category, and it also says all residential rentals must be licensed and all must be registered. Ask rental housing which filing your address needs. Do not ignore the broader sentences.

What does Westminster charge for a rental license?

The inspections page lists a $50 license fee per rental property, an inspection fee of $40 per unit, and $20 per unit for income-restricted units. Registration is described as one time and without a fee. The code separately lists reinspection and no-show fees that council can change.

Are Westminster rentals inspected every year?

The code schedules licensed rental properties by age: not scheduled under six years, every four years from six to twenty, and every two years after twenty, with room to tighten or loosen that gap. Registered dwellings are complaint-based or as conditions warrant, and the city also says they can be inspected proactively.

I live out of state. Do I need someone in Westminster?

The city's license and registration forms say an owner who does not live or keep a permanent business within 50 miles of the rental must name a local agent inside that radius to accept service of process.
