The Erie and Lafayette rental market

Erie has been among the fastest-growing towns in Colorado for years. Lafayette sits immediately west with an older core and more established character. They are adjacent, frequently mentioned together, and behave quite differently as rental markets.

By Nadir Maaiah·August 9, 2026·6 min read

The short answer

  • Erie is predominantly new construction — low maintenance, high HOA prevalence, and rentals often restricted.
  • Lafayette has an older core with more varied stock and generally fewer rental restrictions.
  • Both draw commuter households working in Boulder, Broomfield, Denver and the northwest corridor.
  • Erie spans two counties — Boulder and Weld — which affects taxes and services.
  • Read the covenants before buying in Erie. This is the single most common expensive mistake here.

These two towns get grouped together and should not be. They sit next to each other and offer genuinely different propositions to an owner.

Erie: new stock, and check the HOA

Erie has grown very fast, and most of that growth is recent subdivision construction. For an owner that carries real advantages:

  • Low maintenance. Newer roofs, systems, windows and appliances mean fewer calls and lower capital reserves for years.
  • Efficiency. Current building standards keep tenant utility costs down, which matters at renewal.
  • Amenities. Many subdivisions include pools, trails and parks that make properties easier to lease.
  • Consistent stock. Comparable properties are genuinely comparable, which makes pricing straightforward. See our pricing guide.

And one significant caveat: almost everything in Erie sits in an HOA, and many of those associations restrict rentals — caps on rented units, minimum lease terms, waiting periods after purchase.

This is the most common expensive mistake made in Erie. Buyers close on an investment property and then discover the rental cap is full. Read the recorded covenants during due diligence, and confirm current cap availability rather than whether renting is permitted in principle. See our HOA guide.

Note also that Erie spans Boulder and Weld counties. That affects property tax treatment and county services, and it is worth knowing which side of the line a property sits on.

Lafayette: older, more varied, fewer restrictions

Lafayette has an established core with genuine history alongside newer development. The rental picture differs accordingly:

  • More varied stock — older homes in the core, mid-century neighborhoods, newer subdivisions on the edges.
  • Fewer HOA restrictions, particularly in the older areas, which simply makes renting more straightforward.
  • Closer to Boulder, and priced accordingly.
  • Higher maintenance in the older stock — the trade against Erie's newer inventory.

Lafayette tends to suit an owner who wants Boulder County proximity without Boulder prices and is comfortable with an older building.

Who rents here

Commuter households, overwhelmingly. People working in Boulder, Broomfield, Westminster, Denver or along the northwest corridor who want newer housing, space and good schools at a price that Boulder and Louisville do not offer.

The tenant profile is favorable: stable employment, families, long tenancies. Leasing follows the school calendar rather than the university one, so the strong window is late spring through summer.

What to check before buying

  1. HOA covenants — rental caps, minimum lease terms, waiting periods. In Erie, assume there is an HOA until proven otherwise.
  2. Which county the Erie property sits in, and the tax implications.
  3. Metro district taxes. Many newer Front Range subdivisions carry metropolitan district assessments on top of ordinary property tax. These can be substantial and they materially change a pro-forma. Check before you buy.
  4. Commute reality — how the property actually connects to the employment centers your tenants will use.
  5. City rental requirements — see our city-by-city overview.

The metro district point is worth emphasizing. It catches out-of-area buyers repeatedly, because the assessment is not obvious from a listing and can be large enough to eliminate the cash flow the purchase was based on.

Where they fit

Erie and Lafayette occupy the space between Longmont's yield and Louisville and Superior's stability. Newer stock than either, more restrictions than Longmont, better prices than Louisville.

For an owner who wants low maintenance and a stable commuter tenant base, and who reads the covenants carefully first, they work well.

Frequently asked questions

Can I rent out a house in Erie, Colorado?

Often, but most Erie housing sits in an HOA and many associations restrict rentals through caps, minimum lease terms or waiting periods after purchase. Read the recorded covenants during due diligence and confirm current cap availability, not just whether renting is allowed in principle.

Is Erie or Lafayette better for rental property?

Erie offers newer stock with lower maintenance but heavy HOA prevalence and frequent rental restrictions. Lafayette has more varied, older housing with fewer restrictions and closer Boulder proximity. Erie suits low-maintenance holds; Lafayette suits owners comfortable with an older building.

What are metro district taxes?

Many newer Front Range subdivisions carry metropolitan district assessments in addition to ordinary property tax, funding the district's infrastructure. They can be substantial, are not obvious from a listing, and regularly eliminate the cash flow a purchase was based on. Check before buying.

Which county is Erie in?

Erie spans both Boulder and Weld counties. Which side a property sits on affects property tax treatment and county services, so confirm it during due diligence.

Who rents in Erie and Lafayette?

Predominantly commuter households working in Boulder, Broomfield, Westminster, Denver or the northwest corridor, drawn by newer housing, space and schools. Tenancies tend to be long, and leasing follows the school calendar rather than the university one.
