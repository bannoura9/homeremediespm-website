Landlord fees

Colorado's HB25-1090 Landlord Fee Rules: What's in Force in 2026

Mandatory tenant charges have to be advertised as one number. The markup cap is either 2% or $10 a month, and the Attorney General's utility memo is narrower than it first looks.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·November 21, 2026·8 min read

The short answer

  • HB25-1090 was signed April 21, 2025. The legislature's session-law table and the Division of Real Estate both list January 1, 2026. The act still contains a referendum clause.
  • Charges a renter cannot reasonably avoid have to be advertised as one total price, more prominent than any other price.
  • A third-party markup in the lease is either 2% of the billed amount or $10 a month, not both.
  • Separate bills for common-area maintenance, property taxes, non-rent late fees, services never provided, and the landlord's own legal duties are prohibited.
  • The Attorney General's November 24, 2025 memo is enforcement discretion on utility ratio billing. It does not freeze every older lease.

This is an owner briefing, not legal advice. Have a Colorado attorney read the current text of section 6-1-737 before you renew a lease, answer a tenant demand, or publish a rent number. Where a summary and the act differ, follow the act.

The bill and the date

House Bill 25-1090, Protections Against Deceptive Pricing Practices, was signed April 21, 2025. It added section 6-1-737 to the Colorado Consumer Protection Act and amended the banned-clause list in section 38-12-801. The Division of Real Estate summary and the General Assembly's session-law table both list an effective date of January 1, 2026, chapter 94.

Section 5 says January 1, 2026 applies unless a referendum petition was filed within ninety days after adjournment. This guide found no official notice of a petition. Confirm the text in force before you rewrite a live lease. The same section applies the act to conduct on or after the effective date, which is a different idea from "only leases signed after New Year's Day."

One number, shown more prominently

Section 6-1-737(2)(a) says a person shall not offer, display, or advertise an amount someone may pay unless the total price is disclosed clearly and conspicuously as a single number, without splitting that total into separate fees, and more prominently than any other pricing information.

Subsection (1)(m) defines total price as every amount a person must pay, or cannot reasonably avoid, to use the property, including mandatory add-ons. A trash fee, amenity fee, and admin fee that every applicant pays belong inside that single number, not under a teaser rent.

"Clearly and conspicuously" means noticeable size, contrast, and placement, unavoidable on a screen, in each language the offer uses, and not contradicted by the rest of the ad. Amended section 38-12-801 bars a lease clause that violates section 6-1-737. See our lease requirements guide.

What can sit outside that number

  • A charge the person can reasonably avoid. A storage closet or a parking space the applicant can turn down is not the same thing as a fee every tenant pays. Before someone agrees to an amount outside the total price, subsection (3)(b) still requires a clear disclosure of whether it is refundable, what it is for, and who receives it. Misrepresenting refundability, the charge, the recipient, or the actual price is its own prohibition.
  • A government charge, unless you choose to include it. Subsection (1)(m)(III) excludes taxes and fees imposed on consumers by a government, including a special district. That exclusion is not a property-tax pass-through. The property-tax rule below is a ban.
  • The utility provider's actual cost for the unit. Subsection (2)(d) says the total-price disclosure does not have to include that bill. It does not allow a markup. The markup rule is separate.

Two percent or ten dollars, not both

Section 38-12-801(3)(a)(VI) allows a lease to require either a markup up to two percent of what the third party billed the landlord, or a markup up to a total of ten dollars per month, but not both. Subsections 6-1-737(4)(a) and (4)(g) use that same exception. An illustration, not a recommended charge: on a $50 vendor bill, two percent is $1. You do not add the two percent and then add $10, and the ten dollars is a monthly total, not ten dollars per line.

Charges section 6-1-737(4) says not to require

A landlord or the landlord's agent shall not require a tenant to pay a fee, charge, or amount:

  • For utilities above the provider's charge for service to the dwelling, other than the markup exception.
  • That increases by more than two percent over a rental agreement of one year or less, except the cost of utilities to the dwelling.
  • Related to the payment of property taxes.
  • To process rent or other payments, if a method that costs the tenant nothing is not reasonably accessible. The statute does not say every card fee is banned, and it does not say a card fee is fine because a check exists in theory. The test written down is reasonable access.
  • For overdue payment of an amount that is not rent. A late charge on a late charge, or on an unpaid utility or pet amount, is the pattern. Rent late fees are a different statute, covered in our late-fee and rent guide.
  • For a good, service, or property needed to meet the landlord's own duties, including a habitable home under section 38-12-503. The response clock is in our habitability guide.
  • Above the price of the good or service, other than the 2% or $10 markup.
  • For something you did not actually provide.
  • For maintenance of common areas.
  • That otherwise violates section 6-1-737.

Ratio billing, and leases signed before 2026

On November 24, 2025, the Colorado Department of Law issued a memo to landlords and property managers on utility-billing enforcement. It is on the Attorney General's site: 2025.11.24-1090-Enforcement-Priorities-Memo.pdf. It is department guidance about subsection (4)(a). It is not a court opinion, and it does not amend the statute.

The memo is about ratio utility billing, splitting a master meter among units. The Department says it will enforce section 6-1-737(4)(a) "in a manner that does not pursue legal actions against landlords that allocate utility costs among tenants using a ratio utility billing system," but only if the tenants' bills together do not exceed the provider's charge for the property; there is no markup, surcharge, or admin fee above actual charges except as section 38-12-801(3)(a)(VI) allows; common-area utility costs are left out; and the lease discloses a reasonable and objectively fair method.

The memo then says the Department "will not enforce § 6-1-737(4)(a), C.R.S. retroactively, pursuant to Section 5 (applicability clause) of House Bill 25-1090," and that "any enforcement actions taken by the Department will be pertaining to leases amended, renewed, or entered into on or after the January 1, 2026, Effective Date."

That paragraph is about the Department's own enforcement of subsection (4)(a). It does not say the rest of the statute leaves older leases alone. The act applies to conduct on or after the effective date, which can include a 2026 ad, a 2026 bill, or a 2026 renewal of an older lease. Ask counsel. The Department's discretion does not bind a tenant, a city, or a court.

Before the next renewal

Anything the tenant cannot reasonably avoid goes in the advertised number. A management fee the owner pays a manager is not, by itself, a tenant charge. Putting it on the tenant as a mandatory extra is a question for the lawyer reviewing the lease. This company's published pricing is on the pricing page, and a change on that page does not rewrite an agreement already signed. If a tenant sends the demand in section 6-1-737(5), the statute describes fourteen days, reimbursement, stopping the charge, and 18 percent interest compounded annually if you refuse. Do not answer that letter from this page.

Frequently asked questions

When did Colorado's HB25-1090 fee rules take effect?

The Division of Real Estate and the General Assembly's session-law table both list January 1, 2026. The act says that date applies unless a referendum petition was filed. This guide found no official notice of a petition. Confirm the text in force before you change a lease.

Does the advertised total price have to include utilities?

Section 6-1-737(2)(d) says the disclosure need not include the utility provider's actual charge for service to the unit. Charges above that bill are still limited by subsection (4)(a), aside from the markup exception in section 38-12-801(3)(a)(VI).

Can a lease charge both a 2% markup and $10 a month?

No. Section 38-12-801(3)(a)(VI) allows either a markup up to 2% of the amount the landlord was billed, or a markup up to a total of $10 per month, but not both.

Are leases signed before 2026 exempt?

Do not assume that. The act applies to conduct on or after the effective date. The Attorney General's November 24, 2025 memo limits the Department's enforcement of the utility subsection, 6-1-737(4)(a), to leases amended, renewed, or entered on or after January 1, 2026. That is discretion about one subsection, not a pass for the rest of the statute.

Can I charge a fee to pay rent by card?

Section 6-1-737(4)(d) bars a processing fee if a cost-free method is not reasonably accessible to the tenant. Whether your check, bank-transfer, or portal option meets that test is a question for your attorney.
