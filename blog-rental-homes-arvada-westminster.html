Renter guide

Rental homes in Arvada & Westminster, CO: a 2026 renter's guide

We manage houses, townhomes and condos in both cities, so we field the same questions every week: what does a rental actually cost here, which neighborhoods are worth looking at, and what do I need to get approved? Here are the straight answers.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·October 14, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

  • Westminster rents are a touch higher: the median across all property types is around $2,095 a month as of fall 2026. Arvada averages roughly $1,790 to $1,827.
  • Those are blended figures. A three-bedroom house in either city usually rents for $2,700 to $3,000 or more; a two-bedroom unit lands near $1,900 to $2,100; a one-bedroom around $1,650 to $1,750.
  • Colorado law is on the renter's side in several places: application fees are capped at actual cost, the income requirement cannot exceed twice the rent, security deposits are capped at two months' rent, and pet deposits top out at $300.
  • Homes that are priced to the current market lease in days, not weeks. If you find one you like, have your documents ready before you tour.

Arvada and Westminster sit side by side in the north-west corner of the Denver metro, about 15 to 20 minutes from downtown Denver and 30 minutes from Boulder. They share a renter pool, a light-rail line and a lot of the same employers, but they rent a little differently. This guide covers what renters pay in each city right now, where to look, and how to put yourself at the front of the line when the right home comes up.

What rental homes cost in Arvada and Westminster right now

City-wide averages mix small apartments with large houses, so they understate what a family-sized home costs and overstate what a studio costs. Here is how the 2026 numbers break down by home type, using the same trackers we use to price the properties we manage.

Two things to know about those ranges. First, Arvada rents have been softening gently through 2026 while Westminster's have crept up, so the gap between the two has widened a little. Second, condition matters more than it did two years ago. With more homes on the market, a well-kept house with a finished basement and a two-car garage rents at the top of the range, and a tired one sits. Where renters look in Arvada

  • Olde Town Arvada. The walkable historic core, with the G Line station, restaurants and the farmers market. Mostly older bungalows, small apartment buildings and newer townhomes near the station. Rents here carry a premium for walkability.
  • West Arvada: Candelas, Leyden Rock and West Woods. Newer single-family subdivisions on the west side with larger homes, newer schools and quick access to open space. This is where most of the four-bedroom houses are.
  • Central Arvada around Ralston Valley and Lake Arbor. Established 1970s and 1980s ranch and two-story homes on bigger lots. Good value for families who want a yard and a garage without the newest finishes.
  • Near Standley Lake. Quiet neighborhoods on the Arvada and Westminster line with trails and lake access. Mostly single-family houses.

Where renters look in Westminster

  • Legacy Ridge and The Ranch. Golf-course communities with larger single-family homes. This is the top of Westminster's rental market.
  • Bradburn Village. New-urbanist neighborhood with townhomes, row homes and single-family houses within walking distance of shops. Popular with renters who want a newer home without a long commute.
  • Westminster Station and the old downtown. Newer apartments and townhomes near the B Line commuter-rail station, about 15 minutes to Union Station by train.
  • South Westminster and the 80030 area. Older, more affordable homes and duplexes. The best place to find a house under $2,500 in the city.
  • Northwest Westminster near Standley Lake and Westminster Hills. Established neighborhoods with 1980s and 1990s homes, good schools and the off-leash dog park at Westminster Hills.

What you need to qualify

Every manager has a written screening standard. Ours looks at income, credit, rental history and background, and we apply it the same way to every applicant. Colorado law sets guardrails on what a landlord can ask for, and knowing them helps you walk in prepared.

  • Income. A landlord cannot require income of more than twice the monthly rent. For a $2,400 house that means household income of at least $4,800 a month. Pay stubs, an offer letter or two years of tax returns if self-employed cover it.
  • Application fee. Colorado limits the fee to the landlord's actual cost of running the screening, and the landlord has to give you a receipt. Fees in the $30 to $50 range are normal; much more than that is a question worth asking.
  • Portable screening report. If you have had a tenant screening report run in the last 30 days, Colorado lets you reuse it at other properties and the landlord cannot charge you another application fee for it. Ask for a copy the first time you apply.
  • Security deposit. Capped at two months' rent statewide. One month is typical for the homes we manage. It must be returned, with an itemized list of any deductions, within the deadline set by Colorado law. See our security deposit guide for the full rules.
  • Renters insurance. Most leases require it. It costs about $12 to $25 a month and covers your belongings and liability. Have a quote ready; it is one less thing to sort before move-in.
  • References. Current and previous landlord contact information. A good reference from your last landlord carries real weight when two applications are close.

Pets, deposits and the rules that protect renters

Many of the homes we manage welcome pets with screening. Colorado caps what a landlord can charge for them: a refundable pet deposit of no more than $300, and pet rent of no more than $35 a month or 1.5 percent of rent, whichever is greater. Service animals and emotional support animals are not pets under the law and cannot be charged a deposit or pet rent. Our pet policy guide explains how owners decide.

A few other Colorado rules worth knowing as a renter: late fees are capped at $50 or 5 percent of rent, whichever is greater, and cannot be charged until rent is at least seven days late. Rent can only be raised once in a 12-month period, with proper written notice. Landlords must give reasonable notice before entering, except in an emergency. And a landlord cannot turn you down because you use a housing voucher. Our Colorado landlord-tenant law guide walks through all of it.

How to get the home you want

  • Apply the same day you tour. Correctly priced homes in both cities get multiple applications within 48 hours. Managers generally process complete applications in the order received.
  • Make the application complete. Missing pay stubs or a landlord phone number that goes nowhere pushes you behind the next applicant.
  • Be ready to move within two to four weeks. Owners prefer a start date close to the available date. A 60-day-out move-in is a hard sell on a vacant home.
  • Ask what is included. Lawn care, snow removal, trash, and water are handled differently house to house. In HOA communities some services come with the home. Get it in the lease.
  • Watch for scams. A real manager will show you the home in person or by a scheduled video walk-through, never ask for a deposit before you have seen it, and will have a verifiable business name, phone number and office. If a price looks far below everything else on this page, it is almost certainly not real.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to rent a house in Arvada or Westminster, CO in 2026?

As of fall 2026, Westminster's median rent across all property types is around $2,095 a month and Arvada's average is roughly $1,790 to $1,827. Those figures blend apartments and condos with houses. A three-bedroom single-family house in either city typically rents for about $2,700 to $3,000 or more, two-bedroom units run near $1,900 to $2,100, and one-bedrooms around $1,650 to $1,750.

What do I need to qualify for a rental home in Colorado?

Most managers verify income, credit, rental history and background. Colorado law caps the income a landlord can require at twice the monthly rent, limits application fees to the landlord's actual screening cost, requires the landlord to accept a portable tenant screening report issued within the last 30 days, and caps the security deposit at two months' rent. Having pay stubs, photo ID, landlord references and a renters insurance quote ready speeds up approval.

Are pets allowed in Arvada and Westminster rentals?

It depends on the owner, but Colorado limits what a landlord can charge for a pet: a refundable pet deposit of no more than $300 and pet rent of no more than $35 a month or 1.5 percent of rent, whichever is greater. Assistance animals are not pets and cannot be charged for. Many of the homes we manage are pet-friendly with screening.
