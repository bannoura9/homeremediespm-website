Why automated rent estimates miss

Every listing site offers an instant rent estimate, and they are useful as a starting point. They are built from public records — bedroom count, bathroom count, square footage, lot size, sale history — and a model trained on the surrounding area.

What that model cannot see is most of what decides your rent:

  • Condition and finish level. Two identical floor plans on the same street can be several hundred dollars apart on rent because one has an updated kitchen and the other has the original one.
  • What a tenant actually experiences. Natural light, layout, noise from an arterial road, whether the yard is usable, whether parking is genuinely convenient.
  • Which comparables are real. Automated tools frequently include properties that were listed and never leased. A property that sat for two months at $2,400 is evidence of what the market will not pay.
  • Timing. The same house lists for meaningfully different rent in June than in November.
  • Local rules. Licensing, inspection requirements and occupancy limits all affect what you can lawfully do with the property, and therefore what it is worth.

How we work out the number

  1. Leased comparables, not listed ones. We start from properties that actually signed a lease in the last few months, as close to your property as the data allows.
  2. Adjust for the real differences — square footage, bed and bath count, garage, finished basement, yard, condition and finish level.
  3. Check days on market for each comparable. A house that leased in five days was underpriced; one that took seven weeks was overpriced. That spread is where the right number sits.
  4. Apply the seasonal adjustment for when you would actually be listing.
  5. Sanity-check against current supply — what a tenant searching today would see alongside your property.

Then we tell you the range and where in it we would price, and why.

The cost of getting it wrong

This is worth being concrete about, because owners consistently underestimate one side of it.

Priced too high, the property sits. On a $2,200 rental, three extra weeks of vacancy is about $1,500 — and you generally end up leasing near the correct number anyway, having paid for the experiment. Worse, a listing that goes stale attracts lower-quality inquiries.

Priced too low, you lease quickly and feel good about it, then carry the shortfall for the entire tenancy. $100 a month under market across a two-year tenancy is $2,400, and Colorado limits you to one increase in any 12-month period, so correcting it takes time.

Both errors cost roughly the same. Only one of them feels like a mistake at the time.

Frequently asked questions

How much can I rent my house for in Denver?

It depends on location, size, condition, finish level and the season you list in. The reliable method is comparing properties that actually leased recently within a tight radius — not properties currently listed, which may be priced wrong, and not an automated estimate, which cannot see your condition or finishes. Our analysis is free and usually back within one business day.

Is a rental analysis really free?

Yes, and there is no obligation to use us. We give you the number, how we arrived at it, and what would move it. Plenty of people take the analysis and self-manage, which is a legitimate outcome.

How is this different from Zillow's Rent Zestimate?

Automated estimates work from public records and cannot see your kitchen, your finishes, your yard, your parking or the condition of your systems. They also cannot tell you that a lease ending in November will cost you weeks of vacancy. On a typical Front Range home the gap between an automated figure and the achievable rent is frequently a few hundred dollars a month in either direction.

What do you need from me?

The property address, and a way to reach you. If you can add bedroom and bathroom count, square footage, and anything notable about condition or recent updates, the figure will be tighter. We will ask if we need more.

Will I get sales calls?

One follow-up to make sure you got the analysis and to answer questions. If you tell us you are self-managing or just curious, that is the end of it.
