Furnished stays

Mid-term furnished rentals in the Denver metro, and the 30-day line

A furnished month-plus stay is not a weekend rental and not automatically a 12-month lease. The city's day count, the license, and the written agreement each do different work.

By Nadir Maaiah, Home Remedies Property Management · Westminster, CO·November 21, 2026·8 min read

The short answer

  • A furnished stay of a month or more is a different product from a weekend rental and from a 12-month lease. Length alone does not decide which rules apply.
  • Westminster draws the short-term line at under 30 days. Denver's own pages use "less than 30 consecutive days," "one to 29 days," and "fewer than 29 nights." Arvada's ordinance says less than 30 consecutive calendar days, and a portion of a day counts as a full day.
  • Crossing that line does not end regulation. Denver licenses rentals of 30 days or more. Westminster still registers or licenses the dwelling.
  • The Division of Real Estate's Commission Position 12 says a stay longer than 30 days is not automatically a lease.

Reading this as a guest? This guide is for owners. City licensing pages, not a listing, tell you whether a stay is legal. Own the home? Ask for a free rental analysis before you furnish it for month-plus stays.

Owners in Westminster, Arvada, Thornton, Northglenn, Broomfield, and north Denver get asked for furnished stays longer than a vacation and shorter than a year. Contract clinicians, households between a sale and a purchase, people whose own kitchen is under construction, employees on a defined assignment, and families in town for medical care are the usual reasons. Those are categories of inquiry, not a demand forecast and not a rent quote. This page does not invent occupancy or rates.

Three products that get marketed as one

A short-term rental is priced and regulated like lodging: a license or permit in the cities that require one, lodging or accommodations tax, and a guest who expects hotel-like turnover. A 12-month lease is a tenancy: a security deposit, habitability duties, and, in Denver and Westminster, a long-term rental license or registration. A furnished stay of 30, 60, or 90 days sits in the middle, and owners get in trouble by picking the label that is convenient instead of the label the ordinance uses.

A bed in the house does not pick the label. The city's day count, the written agreement, and how much control you keep are what matter. City-by-city rules for Westminster, Arvada, and Denver are in our STR rules guide. Boulder is a different code, covered in the Boulder short-term rental guide. Operational help starts at STR management, with the warning that the city may call the stay something else.

Where the 30-day line actually sits

Westminster defines a short-term rental as lodging for less than 30 days. A stay that is not under 30 days is outside that license. It is not outside the city's rental program. Single-family homes and duplexes are in the registration track, and properties with four or more units are in the license track, as explained in our Westminster rental license guide. The city's own page also contains broader "all must be licensed" and "all must be registered" sentences. Use the live page, and ask rentalhousing@westminsterco.gov which track your address is on.

Denver's short-term rental page says a short-term rental is available for one to 29 days, that a license is required for fewer than 29 nights at one time, and that renting only for 30-plus days requires a residential rental property license instead. The city's tax guide and the short-term ordinance use "less than 30 consecutive days." The residential-rental FAQ says a license is required for a residence of 30 days or more, and in another sentence says "more than 30 days." Those phrases are not identical. Before you advertise a 29-night or 30-night furnished stay, ask Denver licensing which license that exact length needs. Our Denver rental license guide covers the long-term license. Primary-residence rules apply to Denver short-term rentals and do not, on the city's residential-rental pages, apply the same way to a 30-day-or-more rental.

Arvada Ordinance 4917, effective May 1, 2026, defines a short-term rental as a furnished dwelling rented for less than 30 consecutive calendar days, "counting portions of days as full calendar days." The ordinance also says nothing in that section precludes a rental of 30 or more consecutive days. The program rules describe an STR period of 2 to 29 consecutive days. Some owner checklists shorten this to "31 nights." That phrase is not on the ordinance or the rules page. Because a partial day counts as a full calendar day, a checkout morning can change the count. Confirm the arithmetic with the city before you advertise a stay as exempt. Thornton, Northglenn, and Broomfield write their own rules; this article does not state them. Start with the city license guide and the city page for Thornton, Northglenn, or Broomfield.

The agreement is not a label you print on the ad

Colorado Division of Real Estate Commission Position 12, Short-Term Rentals, recodified from the old CP-19 on June 7, 2022, is written for brokers. It is not a substitute for a city ordinance or for a court's view of a tenancy. It is still the state's clearest official discussion of the middle ground.

The position says a short-term rental is typically a license to use property, like a hotel stay, and is not real estate brokerage. A lease, by contrast, creates an exclusive interest that generally takes a court to end. It also says taxes are generally collected on rental periods of 30 days or less, and then this: "While taxing authorities consider rental periods of 30 days or less as short-term rentals, stays longer than 30 days are not automatically considered a lease." Length is one factor. Others the position names are regular use of the property, the agreement, the user's expectation of privacy, whether rights in the property were conveyed, and how much control the owner keeps. A property marketed as a short-term rental can host a guest for longer than 30 days without that booking, by itself, converting a license into a lease.

Read that with Commission Position 15 on leasing and property management if someone will be paid to place or manage the occupant. Leasing and ongoing management for compensation are brokerage activities under Colorado law, and they have to be done in the brokerage firm's name. Position 12 does not give an unlicensed helper a pass because the stay is "corporate housing."

HOA, insurance, furniture, and wear

A city license does not override covenants. Many associations ban stays under 30 days, and some set a six- or twelve-month minimum that would also bar the mid-term product. Read the declaration before you buy furniture. Our HOA rental restrictions guide is the checklist. Westminster's short-term page says HOA compliance is the owner's job. Arvada's permit is a city permission, not an HOA permission.

Call the insurer before the first guest and ask, in writing, whether a furnished occupant of 30 to 90 days is covered as a tenant, treated as lodging, or excluded. Do not assume a landlord policy written for a one-year lease covers hotel-style turnover, and do not assume a homeowner policy covers either. Furniture, linens, and kitchenware are usually outside a policy that insures only the building. Ask what contents limit you would need. This is a question for the carrier, not a coverage opinion.

A 12-month tenant settles in. A furnished 60-day stay turns the mattress, sofa, and towels several times a year. Budget that replacement, and photograph an inventory at each turnover. Our inspection guide is the method. If the agreement is a tenancy, Colorado's deposit rules apply. If Position 12's factors point to a license, have an attorney write that document instead of scratching dates off a year-long lease. Someone living there under a lease still gets heat, water, and a safe entry, covered in the habitability guide.

Read the covenants, then the city page for the length you intend to offer, including Denver's wording conflict if the house is there. Frequently asked questions

Is a 45-day furnished stay a short-term rental in Westminster?

Westminster's short-term page defines the use as less than 30 days. A 45-day stay is outside that definition. Registration or a rental license can still apply. Confirm the address with the city.

Does Arvada use a 31-night cutoff?

Ordinance 4917 says less than 30 consecutive calendar days, and it counts a portion of a day as a full day. The program rules say 2 to 29 consecutive days. "31 nights" does not appear on those pages. Ask the city how a checkout morning is counted.

Does a stay over 30 days automatically become a lease?

Commission Position 12 says no. Length is one factor. The agreement, privacy, control, and whether you conveyed an interest in the property matter too. Have an attorney characterize the document.

Do I still need a Denver rental license if I only host 30 days or more?

Denver's short-term page says a residential rental property license is required if you only rent for 30-plus days. The city's FAQ is not worded identically. Confirm the stay length with licensing.
